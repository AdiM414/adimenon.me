2020–2023

The Spark

In 2020, the CZU Complex Fires came close enough to force my family out of our home for nearly three months. Watching what that did to our neighbors and our community stuck with me — and it's the reason wildfire prevention has stayed at the center of my work ever since.

On the engineering side, it started with drones. In 2022, as a middle schooler, I built my first real engineering project — Flying High with Drones — and entered it in the Santa Cruz County Science Fair. I placed 1st in Physics. The following year I built Ultrasonic Eyes, exploring how sound waves could let a machine "see" obstacles it couldn't otherwise detect. That one won 1st place in Physics again, plus an award from IEEE.

Those two projects taught me the thing that's stuck with me since: I don't really understand an idea until I've built a version of it that can fail in front of a judge.

2023–2025

Building & Learning

This is the stretch where things got harder, and more interesting. I joined X Academy Hephaestus Robotics and started competing in the MATE ROV Competition — placing 2nd in Northern California in the Navigator category in 2024, then 2nd again in 2025 in the Ranger category, each time qualifying for Worlds. In Michigan that year, our team took 3rd place at the MATE ROV World Championship.

On the science side, I moved from pure physics into sensors and code: Towards Sensor Fusion (2024) explored combining data from multiple sensors for more reliable readings, and in 2025 I built a project using deep learning to improve the safety of self-driving cars — which also won 1st place in Computer Science at the county fair, plus a Genius Olympiad award. That same year I spent a summer as a software intern at Karr Company, working on the backend of a UK-based traffic violation app, and volunteered with YouthServe on environmental clean-ups and food bank sorting around Santa Cruz.

2026

The Breakthrough

Everything came together this year. As VP of ROV Engineering, I helped lead our team to 1st place in Northern California and then to 1st place at the MATE ROV World Championship in Newfoundland & Labrador, Canada — beating 47 teams from 15 countries. At the county science fair, I earned Best in Show, Senior Division and qualified as an ISEF Finalist. At ISEF itself, I won a First Award of $500 from the Patent and Trademark Office Society. And with Fireguard — the autonomous wildfire detection and suppression system I co-founded with my sister Aria for The Conrad Challenge — I was named a Distinguished Conrad Innovator. Fireguard grew directly out of that 2020 evacuation: a network of sensors and small autonomous units designed to catch and suppress fires in remote, high-risk terrain before they can spread.

I also spent the summer at Stanford University, completing CS106EA: Exploring Artificial Intelligence — building a real foundation in machine learning pipelines, neural networks, and generative AI that's shaping what I want to build next.

I'm also taking Math 207: Linear Algebra through the University of North Dakota for undergraduate credit, alongside a senior-year course load of AP Statistics, AP English Literature, AP Physics C (E&M), AP Microeconomics, and AP Government.
